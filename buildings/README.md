# Vietnam buildings: footprints + heights

One row per building for all of Vietnam: OSM footprints first, Open Buildings (Google + Microsoft) footprints where OSM has none, cut back so that no building stands on a road or alley, and a height per building from Google Open Buildings 2.5D Temporal (2023).

Not used by the travel-time backend. Possible uses on the map side: snapping delivery points to a building, telling apartment towers from tube houses, and building density per area as an input for speed interpolation.

The OSM map itself can then be shipped without buildings (`strip_buildings.py`). The building layer replaces them.


Each area runs pass A, pass B, the 2.5D heights (tiles in manifest order, first valid value wins), the compact encoding and the on-road / overlap check (both must be 0, file under 95 MB) in one worker. Pass B re-nodes footprints that snap rounding made overlap, and the compact layer keeps holes, so the check finds nothing: 0 on roads, 0 overlaps and 0 deleted in `ha_noi_01`, `nghe_an_07`, `gia_lai_09` and `tay_ninh_10` (the four largest areas), for about 10 % more fitting time (3 % of an area's wall time) and 0.05 GB more memory. As a safety net, the check runs on the compact rows (decoded) and the clean rows; every building on a carriageway, and the lower-ranked building of every overlapping pair (pass B order: OSM, then confidence, then larger, then lower id), is deleted from both and the check runs again (up to 3 rounds; still failing = failed area). The count is `dropped_check` in the log line and in `by_area/index.json` (3,332 buildings in 455 of 551 areas before the re-noding and the keyhole rings). The parent commits each passed file alone (`git commit -- <file>`), pushes every 10 commits or 2 minutes (`--no-push` to keep them local), and writes `by_area/index.json` at the end. Failed areas are listed and not committed. State is in `data/buildings/_areas/` (`done/<file>.json` per area, stamped with `BUILD_VERSION` of `build_areas.py`); a re-run skips areas that passed with the current version and builds failed ones, and ones built by older code, again. Raise `BUILD_VERSION` whenever a change alters the output, so the next run rebuilds everything; `--rebuild` builds every area again regardless. On the tiny test box the result equals the sequential `fit_footprints.py` + `heights_25d.py` + `compact_layer.py` path row for row.

Every step can be re-run. The download resumes partial files. The merge works in 0.25 degree cells and skips cells already in `data/buildings/_parts/stats.json`; delete `_parts/` and `_merge.duckdb` to redo it, for example after a new PBF. `extract_osm.py` must be re-run whenever the PBF changes. Ids are assigned once by the merge; every later file keeps them.

## Merge rules (`merge_buildings.py`)

- Every OSM building (`building=*`, not `no`) is kept.
- An Open Buildings footprint is dropped when:
  - **low_conf:** Google confidence is below 0.7. Microsoft footprints have no confidence and are kept.
  - **osm_dup:** more than 20 % of its area is covered by OSM buildings.
  - **water:** more than 30 % of its area lies on OSM water areas.
  - **road:** more than 40 % of its area lies on an OSM carriageway. Bridges and tunnels are excluded.
- Carriageway half-width is lanes × 1.75 m when tagged. Otherwise it goes by class: motorway, trunk and primary 7 m; secondary 5 m; tertiary 4 m; residential, unclassified and living_street 2.5 m; service 1.5 m; links 3.5 m.
- Overlaps are measured against the union of the touching features, in UTM 48N.
- The thresholds and the outcome counts are written to `vn_buildings_merged.meta.json`.

## Off roads, no overlaps, shared walls (`fit_footprints.py`)

No house in Vietnam sticks out into a street or a ngõ, and no two buildings overlap (tube houses share walls, they do not cross). In the raw data both happen: Open Buildings is often 1–2 m off, and the merge keeps footprints that are less than 40 % on a road. The rule applies to OSM and Open Buildings alike. It replaces `clip_to_roads.py`, which only cut; that file now only holds the carriageway definitions.

- **Carriageways:** half-width lanes × 1.5 m when tagged, else motorway/trunk/primary 5 m, secondary 4 m, tertiary 3 m, links 2.5 m, residential/unclassified/living_street 1.5 m, service 1.0 m, footway/path/pedestrian/steps/cycleway 0.75 m, plus 0.1 m for coordinate rounding. Narrow on purpose, because OSM has no width for alleys. Not counted: bridges, tunnels, `covered`, `indoor`, `area=yes`, `highway=construction/proposed/platform/corridor/elevator/bus_stop`.
- **Pass A, shift:** a footprint on a carriageway moves away from it (from the nearest point of the road's centre line toward the footprint's centroid, weighted by overlap area), by the smallest distance up to 3 m that clears every carriageway nearby.
- **Cut, when no shift works** (a house between two alleys, or more than 3 m on the road): footprint minus the carriageways, largest part kept, dropped under 50 % or 6 m².
- **Pass B, shared walls.** Houses in Hanoi and Ho Chi Minh City stand wall to wall, while detected footprints usually stop 0.3–1 m short of each other. Pass B works on the storage grid (1e-6°, what the compact layer keeps), so storing the result moves nothing.
  - **Close narrow gaps:** gaps under 1 m between buildings (closing of their union at 0.5 m, minus carriageways) are filled; each building grows into them by up to 0.5 m.
  - **Share the ground:** the outlines of touching footprints are noded together and cut the ground into faces. Each face goes to the highest-ranked footprint covering it: OSM first, then Open Buildings by confidence (Microsoft after Google), then larger first. Neighbours are built from the same faces, so they share each wall vertex for vertex: no overlap, no gap.
  - **Snap rounding:** noding on the grid moves an outline by up to about one grid unit (0.1 m). Two footprints that stood less than that apart without touching fall in separate groups, are noded separately, and could come out overlapping by a thin sliver (0.05–1 m² seen in the first nationwide areas). Groups whose results overlap are therefore noded again as one group, until no footprint overlaps one from another group (`remerged`). The union of a footprint's faces on the grid can also bend an edge into a neighbour's face; the exact coverage union of the faces is then used instead (`bent_unions`). As a last resort a footprint still overlapping a higher-ranked one gives it the shared ground (`safety_net`). Footprints with no such neighbour are unchanged.
  - A footprint left with under 50 % of its area or under 6 m² was a duplicate and is dropped; its faces go to the next in rank.
- Work runs in 0.25° cells (largest first) on 60 % of the logical CPUs (`--workers` overrides). Pass B reads the pass A result of neighbours in adjacent cells and only looks at a building's surroundings, so a building on a cell edge comes out the same from both sides (tested with 0.005° cells: same counts).
- Output adds `shift_m`, `clip_m2`, `fill_m2`, `overlap_m2`, `road_gap_m` and `nb_gap_m` (distance to the nearest carriageway / other building, up to 5 m).
- **Bùi Xương Trạch test** (11,600 footprints): 2,486 shifted (median 0.82 m; 804 of them OSM), 668 cut, 84 dropped on a road, 4,501 grown into a gap, 5,491 gave ground to a neighbour, 49 duplicates dropped, 11,467 left.
  - `check_road_overlap.py` on the raw footprints: 1,341 overlapping pairs over 0.5 m².
  - With 10 cm between walls (the previous rule): 0 overlaps, but 10,303 pairs 2 cm–1 m apart and 9,722 m² of gaps under 1 m wide.
  - Now: 0 on roads, 0 overlapping pairs over 0.05 m², 9,615 pairs sharing a wall, 79 m² of gaps under 1 m wide left. The same after compression.
  - The 815 pairs still 2 cm–1 m apart: 565 are corner to corner (houses in different rows), 63 are under 10 cm (less than one grid step), 187 are small pockets, 34 m² in all.

## Heights (`heights_25d.py`)

- Source: `gs://open-buildings-temporal-data` (public, no account), band `building_height`, year 2023, licence CC BY 4.0 or ODbL.
- Each tile is read at 1 m through the file's built-in 2× overview: height band only, about 1 km windows, only where buildings are. Nothing is stored except the result.
- On Bùi Xương Trạch (9,692 buildings), 1 m reads stay within 1 m of the full 0.5 m read for 98.6 % of buildings, and within 0.5 m for 93.2 %. At 2 m the figure is 93.4 % within 1 m.
- Height of a building is the median of the pixels inside its (clipped) footprint. A footprint smaller than a pixel takes the pixel under its centroid. 2.5D values under 2 m are treated as "no building".
- Tiles of neighbouring S2 cells overlap, and one of the two is nodata there. A building keeps the first valid value.
- `--workers` (threads reading tiles) defaults to 60 % of the logical CPUs. The nationwide run takes hours. Each finished tile is saved to `data/buildings/_25d_done/`. After an interruption (Windows Update restarted the machine during the first run), re-running resumes from those files. Delete `_25d_done/` when the footprints change.
- Floors: the OSM `building:levels` tag when present. Otherwise `1 + round((height − 3.9) / 3.3)`, at least 1: ground floor 3.9 m, upper floors 3.3 m.
- **building:part heights** (`part_heights.py`): towers are often mapped in OSM as `building:part` ways and relations that carry `height` / `building:levels`, while the outline has none (Keangnam Landmark 72: outline untagged, published at 62.4 m from 2.5D, tower part 336 m). A part belongs to a building when its representative point lies inside the published (decoded) footprint, or at least 50 % of its area does. Part height is `height`, else `building:levels` × 3.3 m plus a plain `roof:height`; values ≤ 0 or over 900 m are ignored. The tallest part replaces the building's height when the building has none or is lower, with `height_src` = `osm_part` and floors = the most of its parts' levels (else from the height, as above). `build_areas.py` and `compact_layer.py` apply the rule on every build (it needs `osm_parts.parquet`); `part_heights.py apply` applied it to the published files in place (October 2026 extract: 2,932 parts, 2,330 with a usable height; 622 buildings in 69 files changed).
- The 2.5D height is a model estimate from Sentinel-2, effective resolution 4 m. Against the three OSM-tagged buildings in Bùi Xương Trạch, it matched one (7 floors, 25.5 m) and missed two. It tends to under-read narrow tube houses.

## Height calibration (`calibrate_heights.py`)

Against 20,883 OSM-tagged buildings the 2.5D value is pulled toward the middle: low readings are too low (2.5D under 4 m: tagged median 6.6 m), readings of 10–30 m too high (15–20 m: tagged median 13.2 m), and towers are under-read (hold-out bias −16 m for buildings over 60 m). `calibrate_heights.py` corrects it with OSM heights (`height`, else `building:levels` × 3.3 m) as truth.

- **Truth** (`truth`): the 35,686 published rows with `height_src` `osm` / `osm_part`; their 2.5D value is read again from the tiles on the published footprint, as `build_areas.py` reads it (the published rows keep only the chosen height). Not used to fit: junk (under 2 m, over 500 m, `height` and `building:levels` disagreeing: 131), mass tags (a 2 km block of ≥ 50 tagged buildings where one value covers ≥ 85 %: 12,365, e.g. `building:levels=4` on 9,000 houses in Lâm Đồng whose 2.5D median is 4 m), and `osm_part` (recorded only where taller than the 2.5D value, so biased).
- **Groups** by footprint, first match: tube (short side < 6.5 m, long / short ≥ 1.8, < 300 m²), small (< 60 m²), house (< 300 m²), large (< 1,500 m²), xlarge. Footprint plus height bands and plus density were tried and did not do better in cross-validation.
- **Model:** per group a weighted Huber line in log space, log(real) = a + b·log(h25), so alpha = real / h25 = e^a·h25^(b−1) changes smoothly with the 2.5D value (a ratio of two small numbers cannot explode). Each 2 km block weighs at most 100 buildings. Local correction: the log residuals of the OSM anchors within 500 m (clipped to ±0.7), weighted by distance and by how similar their 2.5D value is, shrunk toward 0 by one pseudo-anchor. Result clamped to 0.5–3 × h25 and 2–600 m. The grouping, radius and shrinkage are picked by 5-fold block cross-validation on the training rows; a group that does not beat the raw value there stays `g25`.
- **No 2.5D value** (Google sees under 2 m: 15.4 M buildings): per area class (< 60, < 300, ≥ 300 m²) a median regression of log(real) on the IDW (power 1, 50 m) of the raw 2.5D heights of `g25` neighbours in the same file, else the class median; clamped to 3–100 m. `height_src` = `est`.
- **Hold-out** (20 % of the calibration rows as whole 2 km blocks): MAE 6.01 → 5.52 m (1.82 → 1.67 floors), median 3.66 → 3.36 m, bias −0.81 → −1.41 m. Best on tube / small / house (MAE 4.2–5.0 → 3.3–3.7 m), little change on large footprints and above 30 m. No-2.5D buildings: against the one-floor default, MAE 5.09 → 5.21 m on held-out tagged buildings Google missed (a tie: 39 % of them are tagged exactly one floor), 12.99 → 10.47 m on held-out buildings whose 2.5D value is masked. Every number, per group and per height band, is in `height_calibration.json`.
- **Tall stage** (`tall`, after `fit`): the 2.5D error repeats for similar buildings close together, so a building with raw h25 ≥ 30 m takes alpha = weighted median of real / h25 over OSM anchors (clean rows with h25 ≥ 30 m or real ≥ 60 m, 2,102) whose h25 is within ±25 % of its own, from the smallest of 250 / 500 / 1,000 m holding ≥ 2 of them, weighted 1 / (1 + d / 1 km); the height moves half way, in log units, from the main-stage value to alpha × raw h25 (clamps: alpha 0.8–4, 2–600 m). With no 2 such anchors within 1 km it keeps the main-stage value. Google's 2.5D tops out at 99 m and is flat from about 85 m (tagged buildings of 100–200 m read a median 87 m, the 200–461 m towers 25–70 m); for h25 ≥ 94 m every saturated anchor counts as similar. All of it (T, N, weights, radii, fallback, step share, saturation rule, `osm_part` anchors) was chosen by inner block cross-validation; the proposed 2 → 25 km → province → national-band search did worse (hold-out > 60 m: MAE 23.4 m): the correlation of log(real / h25) between similar towers is 0.64 under 200 m, 0.40 at 200–500 m and about 0.1 beyond 1 km, while held-out towers sit a median 870 m from the nearest similar training anchor. `osm_part` anchors did not help in CV (they exist only where taller than 2.5D). Hold-out (same rows as above), main stage → tall stage: overall MAE 5.52 → 5.50 m; 0–10 m unchanged; 10–30 m 5.70 → 5.73 m; 30–60 m 13.33 → 13.04 m (bias −7.59 → −7.74); > 60 m 21.00 → 20.89 m (bias −13.96 → −13.51). The stage does not reach its target for towers (> 60 m clearly better than 21.0 m / −14.0 m bias); it is kept because it helps slightly and moves the tallest towers toward their real heights. A quarter of the > 60 m error is 20 towers that 2.5D reads under 30 m, which no h25-based stage can see. Rows with raw h25 < 30 m keep the main-stage value; `height_src` stays `g25cal`.
- **Apply** rewrites only `height_dm`, `height_src` and `floors` (floors from the new height as above) and the `bytes` in `index.json`; `osm` / `osm_part` rows and every other column stay identical. It works from raw 2.5D values only: a file already holding `g25cal` / `est` is skipped. The raw values are in the `by_area` files of commit `703e019`; export them (`git archive 703e019 buildings/by_area | tar -x`, into `data/buildings/_calib/raw_by_area`) and apply with `--src-dir` there and `--out-dir buildings/by_area`; the same parameters give byte-identical files. `build_areas.py` does not call it: a rebuilt area is uncalibrated until `apply` runs again.

## Wikidata and sibling heights (`extra_heights.py`)

The 2.5D value carries little signal for towers (it saturates at about 99 m), so two sources of real heights are added on top of the calibration. Precedence per building: `osm` / `osm_part` > `wikidata` > `sibling` > `g25cal` > `est`. `build` writes every accepted row (file, id, height, floors, QID or sibling ids, rule) and the validation to the committed `height_sources.json`; `apply` reads it, so the published files follow from that file plus the raw layer, byte for byte, and a calibrated file is skipped. All decisions are fixed rules in `extra_heights.py`; the thresholds are in the JSON.

- **Wikidata** (CC0 1.0; query.wikidata.org, raw results cached in `data/buildings/_wikidata/`): items in Vietnam (P17) or in a box around it with coordinates (P625) and a height (P2048, normalised to metres) or floors above ground (P1101). Height = P2048, else floors × 3.3 + 4 m; floors = P1101, else height / 3.3. Dropped before matching: not a building class, with or without floors (statues, drums, bridges, waterfalls, 'built structure'), a direct class naming a company / organisation, a label naming a cruise / boat / ship, coordinates coarser than 0.001°, a class (P31/P279*) or state of use (P5817) or event (P793) marking it proposed / planned / under construction / unbuilt / cancelled / not in use / demolished, a P576 date, opened after the map extract. Matching: footprints within 50 m of the point; score = 3 × name match with the OSM name (similarity ≥ 0.75), −1 when the footprint carries another name, +1 if it contains the point, +0..1 for size (100–2,000 m²), +1 if its own raw height ≥ min(H / 2, 30 m), − distance / 25 m. Best score wins; a best footprint whose OSM name has similarity < 0.5 rejects the item; a runner-up within 0.25 without a name match rejects it as ambiguous, within 0.75 it is listed; one item per footprint (P2048 items first). Plausibility: height / raw h25 within 0.5–4 (the tall-stage alpha clamp; no upper bound for h25 ≥ 94 m) and within the p1–p99 range of real / h25 of OSM-tagged rows in the claim's height band; height / √area ≤ the p99.5 of tagged buildings ≥ 30 m (percentiles from rows outside the held-out blocks); a footprint without a raw 2.5D value only by name match. The percentile band alone let VietinBank Business Center (363 m, never completed, h25 48 m) through, because 2.5D under-reads the 64 tagged towers ≥ 150 m by up to 13.8×. October 2026: 679 items (54 with P2048, 640 with P1101, mostly hotels), 293 matched, 228 applied (65 matched OSM-tagged buildings and change nothing), 386 rejected (94 ambiguous, 85 no plausible footprint, 49 another OSM name, 39 none within 50 m, 53 implausible, 32 not a building or a vessel, 22 duplicates, 12 other). Against OSM where both exist (59 rows, 6 where the OSM tag looks wrong left out): MAE 18.7 m, median 5.7 m, bias −4.0 m (hotel items inside towers carry the hotel's floors); on the 37 calibration rows Wikidata 12.0 m against 15.8 m for the calibrated 2.5D value. OSM tags are never changed; the suspect ones (e.g. 3.3 m on a 15-floor hotel) are listed in the JSON.
- **Sibling copy:** a building without a real height and with raw h25 ≥ 25 m or a footprint ≥ 400 m² takes the median height of its voting siblings: real-height buildings (`osm` / `osm_part` minus junk and mass-tagged blocks, and `wikidata`) within 300 m, area within ±25 %, long side and elongation within 0.8–1.25 ×, and linked by the same indexed name stem (CT1 / CT2, Times City T1 / T2; generic words such as Tòa, Lô, Nhà, Khu, Dãy, Block are dropped, so 'Tòa A' / 'Tòa B' alone are no link), the same OSM name (not generic-only), the same smallest OSM complex area (landuse residential / commercial / retail, residential=*, school / college / university / hospital grounds, ≤ 0.5 km²) or the same site relation. A source votes only if its own height / raw h25 is within 0.5–4 (h25 ≥ 94 m: ≥ 0.5; a 3.3 m tag on a building 2.5D sees at 32 m is not copied). A target with a raw h25 takes only siblings whose raw h25 (or, without one, real height) is within a factor of it (both ≥ 94 m count as similar), and a complex / site link without a name also needs its raw h25 ≥ 25 m (a complex mixes podiums and towers); a target without a raw h25 takes only siblings linked by name. The factor (1.25 / 1.5 / 1.67 / 2, capped at 2), the minimum count and an agreement check were chosen by leave-one-out on the training blocks with training-side sources: factor 1.67, one sibling, no agreement check (MAE 7.62 m against 9.29 m for the current value). Floors: lower median of the voting siblings' floors. 98 buildings get `sibling` (498 have linked siblings, 400 without a voting one). Leave-one-out over the 6,543 eligible OSM-tagged calibration rows: 872 covered, MAE 15.8 → 4.4 m on them (bias −7.3 → 0.0 m; > 60 m: 491 rows, 21.9 → 4.8 m). Tagged siblings are often tagged together, so this is optimistic.
- **Hold-out** (same 2 km blocks as the calibration; Wikidata where it matches, siblings from training-side heights only): 20 of 4,225 rows covered (3 Wikidata, 17 sibling; siblings sit within 300 m, so mostly in the same held-out block), MAE on them 8.57 → 6.87 m. Per band: 0–10 m 3.00 → 3.00, 10–30 m 5.73 → 5.72, 30–60 m 13.04 → 13.11 m (bias −7.74 → −7.48), > 60 m 20.89 → 20.75 m (bias −13.51 → −13.45).

## Front-row houses: minimum in Hà Nội, alpha everywhere (`frontrow_heights.py`, `alpha_frontrow.py`)

A **domain rule from the user, not a measurement**: almost every house on a Hà Nội street front today has 3 floors or more. The published layer read them far lower (front-row houses, centroid ≤ 20 m from a trunk / primary / secondary / tertiary centre line, < 300 m², ≤ 25 m: median 6.1 m, 83 % at 1–2 floors), because 2.5D under-reads narrow tube houses and OSM levels of front-row houses look under-tagged (Google reads many 1–2-floor-tagged ones at 8–17 m).

- **Front-row house** (`build`, from the raw layer and `osm_roads.parquet`; ids and every constant in the committed `frontrow_heights.json`): a footprint in any file (Hà Nội only until 2026-10-27, the whole country since), 20–300 m², with a street centre line within half-width + 4 m of its outline, and no other footprint crossing the shortest segment from its outline to that line by more than 0.1 m (so second-row houses do not count). Streets: trunk / primary / secondary / tertiary (+ `_link`) at any name, and residential / unclassified / living_street with a name not starting with Ngõ / Ngách / Hẻm / Kiệt (unnamed ones are alleys); no bridges, tunnels, covered / indoor ways or `area=yes`. Half-width: class default (trunk 10, primary 8, secondary 6, tertiary 5, links 4, residential / unclassified / living_street 3 m), or lanes × 1.75 m when tagged and wider (OSM lanes count motor lanes only), clamped to 3–12 m.
- **Rule** (`calibrate_heights.py apply`, after calibration, Wikidata and sibling rows), in the `ha_noi_*` files only (`min_floors_files_prefix`): a listed house whose `height_src` is then `g25cal` or `est` gets height = max(height, 3 × 3.3 = 9.9 m) and floors = max(floors, 3); `height_src` becomes `frontrow` only where that changes it. `osm` / `osm_part` / `wikidata` / `sibling` rows are never touched. Apply needs only the raw layer and the JSON; `--no-frontrow` leaves the rule out.
- **Front-row alpha** (`alpha_frontrow.py`, the user's decision of 2026-10-27, measured against their own street photos with `alpha_vs_photos.py`): first in the 18 Hà Nội ward files, the same day extended by the user to every ward and commune of the country: a listed front-row house that is `g25cal` after calibration and has a raw 2.5D value gets height = min(8 × 3.3 m, max(height, 1.7 × raw 2.5D)); `height_src` becomes `frontrow_alpha` where that lifts it (in Hà Nội only above the 9.9 m minimum, the rest go on to the minimum; elsewhere there is no minimum). Applied before the minimum, in `calibrate_heights.py apply`; `--no-alpha` leaves it out. On Nguyễn Trãi and Tây Sơn the segment medians end within +0.6 / −0.3 m of the photos; on the two Long Biên streets measured it overshoots by 3–5 m. Outside Hà Nội it has not been checked against photos.
- **October 2026:** 160,658 front-row houses in 25 files (of 2.63 M houses of 20–300 m²); 137,435 changed (108,246 from `g25cal`, 29,189 from `est`). Front-row floors before → after: 1 floor 52,184 → 283, 2 floors 75,553 → 323, 3 floors 23,786 → 150,917, 4+ 9,135 unchanged (≥ 3 floors 20.5 % → 99.6 %; median 6.4 → 9.9 m). The 606 left under 3 floors are OSM-tagged (of 1,398 OSM-tagged front-row houses) and stay as tagged. On the wider evidence set above (176,563 rows, which also holds second-row houses and alley fronts): ≥ 3 floors 17.0 % → 65.4 %, median 6.1 → 9.9 m.

## House numbers, interpolated (`housenumber_interp.py`, not published yet)

Every street-front footprint of the Hà Nội files gets a house number by code, one rule for every street; the output stays in the gitignored `data/buildings/_housenumber/<file>` (`id, hn, hn_num, hn_street, hn_src, hn_conf, hn_side, hn_s`) until the user accepts the measurements. Anchors: every OSM node and way with a number in its tags (`points` reads them from the PBF): `addr:housenumber` + `addr:street`, else a number leading `addr:street`, `addr:full`, `addr:housename`, `addr:place` or `name` ("73 ngõ 77 bùi xương trạch" = 73 on Ngõ 77, "73/1 ngõ 77 …" = 1 on Ngách 77/73); and alley mouths at every level ("Ngõ 85 Phố X" = 85 on Phố X, "Ngách 77/50" = 50 on Ngõ 77, "Hẻm 77/10/2" = 2 on Ngách 77/10); OCR reads are not used (2 of 28 right) and the street-photo truth has no coordinates. Streets are named OSM ways joined by unaccented name and alley path; a footprint belongs to the nearest named way it faces within half-width + setback (10 m on trunk / primary / secondary / tertiary, 5 m on named residential ways, 4 m on alleys: the p75 of OSM-addressed facades on dev streets) with no other footprint in between (front-row test of `frontrow_heights.py` for every named way). Per street side: direction from the anchors' Theil-Sen slope, parity from the side's anchors (the other side's opposite when it has none), anchors of the wrong parity or out of order dropped, linear interpolation between kept anchors rounded to the parity and kept strictly inside, letter suffixes (at most C) when a gap has more houses than numbers, extrapolation up to 150 m at low confidence. Alleys without anchors use the rule measured on dev alleys (numbers rise from the mouth 90 %, odd on the left looking in 84 %), low confidence. Hold-out by street (street names split by hash; every anchor dropped in turn, all Hà Nội, October 2026): interpolation between anchors ≤ 60 m apart 77 % exact, 97 % within 4, p90 2; all anchors 63 % exact, p90 6; extrapolation p90 18; the alley rule without anchors only 8 % exact, median 8, p90 32. With the 2026-10-28 PBF and the text rules: 21,408 OSM anchors (260 from text fields), 14,380 alley mouths (levels 1-5: 8,163 / 5,048 / 1,100 / 66 / 3); hold-out unchanged (all 62 % exact, p90 6; alleys 61 %, p90 6). Matching addresses to any way of a street instead of its axis was measured worse (p90 12) and is not used.

## Compact layer `data/buildings/vn_buildings_compact.parquet` (`compact_layer.py`)

Footprint plus height in about 27 bytes per building (Bùi Xương Trạch test: 26.8 B; 18.9 B before overlaps were removed and walls shared, when touching tube houses could still be rectangles), zstd level 19. `decode()` in `compact_layer.py` turns a row back into a polygon.

- **Rectangle**, when the footprint fills ≥ 95 % of its rotated bounding rectangle, has no hole, and the rectangle cannot reach a road or another building: its largest bulge past the footprint plus 0.15 m is less than `road_gap_m` and `nb_gap_m` (5 m when nothing is recorded within 5 m). `build_areas.py` decodes every area and stores a rectangle that still reaches a road or a neighbour by more than 0.001 m² (two rectangles bulging into the same gap) as its ring (`rect_to_ring`). Stored as centre (`lon_e6`, `lat_e6`), `length_dm`, `width_dm` and `angle_cdeg` (long side, clockwise from north). Round-trip IoU median 0.97.
- **Ring** otherwise, including every footprint that was cut: first vertex in `lon_e6`/`lat_e6`, then int16 (dx, dy) steps in 1e-6 degrees, one ring. A footprint with holes (a courtyard, or ground pass B gave to an OSM building or a road inside it) is stored as a keyhole ring: each hole is joined to the outer ring by a zero-width slit between a vertex of each, walked there and back. `decode()` sees the repeated vertices and rebuilds the holes exactly (`make_valid` of the ring, largest polygon). A reader with its own decoder must do the same: the raw keyhole ring is an invalid polygon (its slits have zero width), though its area is already right. Before, holes were dropped, and the filled footprint overlapped the buildings standing in them, so the check deleted large footprints (11,920 m² in `ha_noi_01`). Round-trip IoU median 0.99.
- Plus `id`, `src`, `height_dm`, `height_src`, `floors`.

## Download files by area (`group_communes.py`, `split_by_area.py`)

A client downloads and opens only the area it needs: `data/buildings/by_area/<province>_<NN>.parquet`, same schema as the compact layer, plus `by_area/index.json`.

- **Boundaries** come from OSM, two-level system since July 2025: 34 provinces (`admin_level=4`, `ISO3166-2` VN-*) and 3,318 communes (`admin_level=6` named Xã / Phường / Đặc khu), assembled once into `data/buildings/vn_admin.parquet`. The extract cuts off part of the offshore boundary of Trường Sa, so osmium cannot close Khánh Hòa or Trường Sa; for any unit it cannot assemble, the script polygonizes the member ways present and keeps the closed faces (Khánh Hòa: 46 of 61 ways, mainland and islands kept).
- **Groups** (`group_communes.py`): neighbouring units of one province and one kind, up to 3 urban wards (Phường) or up to 10 communes (Xã, Đặc khu) per file, at most about 15 MB. Small groups merge into a neighbour of the same kind. Result: 551 files, median 1.5 MB, largest about 9.4 MB, about 1 GB in total (estimates).
- **Table of contents**, committed: [`commune_groups.md`](commune_groups.md) (readable, per province) and `commune_groups.json` (machine-readable). Grouping depends only on boundaries and rough counts, so it does not change when footprints are refined.
- **Assignment** (`split_by_area.py`): a building goes to the commune that contains its centroid, so each building is in exactly one file and keeps its id. Centroids outside every commune go to the nearest one (nationwide: 64,432, 0.12 %).
- `by_area/index.json` lists per file its province, communes with exact building counts, bytes and lon/lat bbox. A client picks files by bbox or by commune name.
- Bùi Xương Trạch test: 11,510 buildings in 3 files (`ha_noi_15` Khương Đình, `ha_noi_16` Phương Liệt, `ha_noi_19` Định Công), totals match the compact layer.

## OSM without buildings (`strip_buildings.py`)

Writes `osrm/data/vietnam-nobuildings.osm.pbf`: drops `building` / `building:part` ways and relations, their untagged member ways, and untagged nodes used only by dropped ways. For the October 2026 extract: 331 MB → 297 MB. Building ways are about 10 % of the file, so the map does not get much lighter; the building layer is where the volume is. OSRM keeps reading `vietnam-latest.osm.pbf`.
